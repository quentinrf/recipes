# Burrata with Pistachio Pesto and Chili Honey

*Fresh burrata set over a bright, herb-laced pistachio spread, finished with toasted crushed pistachios and a warm chili honey that cuts cleanly through all that cream.*

**Serves:** 4
**Prep:** 20 min | **Cook:** 5 min | **Total:** 25 min

---

## Ingredients

### Pistachio Pesto
- 120g (4 oz) shelled raw unsalted pistachios, divided — 80g for the pesto, 40g for the garnish
- 1 small garlic clove, grated
- 15g (½ oz) fresh basil leaves (or half basil, half mint for a brighter note)
- 30g (1 oz) Parmigiano Reggiano, finely grated
- 60ml (¼ cup) good extra-virgin olive oil, plus more to finish
- Zest of 1 lemon + 1 tsp lemon juice
- Flaky sea salt and freshly cracked black pepper

### Chili Honey
- 60ml (¼ cup) good-quality honey — Sicilian wildflower or acacia
- ½ tsp Calabrian chili flakes (or Aleppo pepper for gentler, fruitier heat)
- 1 tsp lemon juice

### To Serve
- 2 × 125g balls fresh burrata, at room temperature
- Flaky sea salt (Maldon)
- Extra-virgin olive oil
- Fresh basil or small mint leaves
- Grilled bread or crostini (optional, but do it)

---

## Method

### Blanch and skin the pistachios
Bring a small pot of water to a boil. Drop in all 120g of pistachios for **45 seconds** — you're loosening the skins, not cooking the nuts. Drain immediately and tip onto a clean kitchen towel; rub vigorously until the skins slip off. This step is non-negotiable if you want a vivid green pesto. Skip it and you get brown paste.

Set aside 40g of the best-looking blanched pistachios for the garnish.

### Toast the garnish pistachios
In a dry skillet over medium heat, toast the reserved 40g pistachios for 3–4 minutes, tossing frequently, until fragrant and lightly golden. Pull them before they darken — past golden they turn bitter. Season with a pinch of flaky salt while still warm, then transfer to a board and crush roughly with the flat of a knife. You want uneven rubble — coarse chunks and fine crumbs — not uniform dust. Set aside.

### Make the pistachio pesto
In a food processor, combine the 80g blanched pistachios, garlic, basil, Parmigiano, lemon zest, and lemon juice. Pulse 8–10 times to break everything down into a coarse rubble. With the machine running, stream in the olive oil slowly. Stop as soon as you have a thick, spreadable paste with visible texture — this is a spread, not a sauce. Taste: it should be nutty, sharp from the lemon, and savoury. Adjust salt. If it's too stiff, loosen with a teaspoon of water or a little more oil. Make this within an hour of serving; lemon slows oxidation but won't stop it.

### Make the chili honey
In a very small saucepan over low heat, warm the honey with the chili flakes for 2–3 minutes, just until it loosens and the chili blooms in the warmth. Do not let it boil. Off the heat, stir in the lemon juice — this is what stops it tasting flat and cloying. Taste: sweet up front, slow heat behind, a little brightness at the end. It will stay pourable at room temperature.

---

## Assembly / To Serve

Spoon the pistachio pesto generously across a serving plate or board — go thick, spreading it into an uneven layer so the burrata has something to sink into. Nestle the two burrata balls on top. **Do not cut them.** Let your guests break the skin at the table; the moment the cream spills into the pesto is the point of the dish.

Scatter the crushed toasted pistachios over and around. Drizzle the chili honey directly over the burrata and across the spread — be generous, don't be timid. Finish with a final drizzle of good olive oil, a few flakes of Maldon, a crack of black pepper, and torn basil or mint leaves.

Serve immediately. Have grilled bread alongside for scooping.

---

## Tips & Notes

- **Burrata temperature.** Pull it from the fridge 30–45 minutes before plating. Cold burrata is dense, rubbery, and won't yield when broken. Room temperature, it collapses into cream. There is no shortcut here.
- **Pistachio quality.** Sicilian Bronte pistachios — if you can source them — are worth every extra penny. Intensely flavoured, deeply green, incomparably aromatic. Regular pistachios work; Bronte pistachios make a statement.
- **Make-ahead.** The pesto keeps refrigerated for up to 3 days — press cling film directly onto the surface to slow oxidation. The chili honey keeps indefinitely at room temperature. Assemble only at the last moment.
- **Heat level.** Calabrian chili gives clean, direct heat. Aleppo pepper is milder and fruitier — better if you're serving guests with lower heat tolerance. A pinch of both is not wrong.
- **Wine.** Vermentino, Falanghina, or a good Sicilian white — something dry, mineral, and lively. Avoid anything oaked; it fights the pistachio.
