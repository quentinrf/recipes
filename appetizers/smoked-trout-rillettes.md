# Smoked Trout Rillettes with Rye Toast, Pickled Shallot & Dill

*A classic French spread built around good smoked fish — rich, sharp, and honest. No cooking required beyond toasting bread, which is exactly why it earns its place on a boat.*

**Serves:** 6–8
**Prep:** 20 min | Pickle: 30 min (or up to 2 days ahead) | **Total:** 20 min active

---

## Components

1. Pickled shallots — start these first, they only improve with time
2. Rillettes base
3. Toasted rye

---

## Ingredients

### Rillettes
- 8 oz (225g) smoked trout, skin and pin bones removed, flaked
- 4 oz (115g) cream cheese, room temperature
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
- 1 tbsp prepared horseradish (more to taste)
- 1 tbsp lemon juice, plus zest of half the lemon
- 2 tbsp finely chopped chives
- 1 tbsp chopped dill, plus extra fronds for garnish
- Dash of hot sauce or pinch of cayenne (optional)
- Salt and black pepper

### Pickled Shallots
- 1 large shallot, sliced into thin rings
- 1/2 cup (120ml) white wine vinegar or rice vinegar
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/4 cup (60ml) water

### To Serve
- 1 loaf rye bread, sliced and cut into small squares
- Olive oil, for brushing
- Extra dill fronds, black pepper

---

## Method

### Pickle the shallots
Heat vinegar, sugar, salt, and water in a small pot just until dissolved — you're not trying to boil it, just get the sugar and salt into solution. Pour over the shallot rings in a jar, let cool to room temperature, then lid and refrigerate. Usable at 30 minutes; noticeably better after a day. Do this first — everything else is faster.

### Build the rillettes
While the shallots sit, mash the cream cheese and butter together until smooth, then fold in the flaked trout, horseradish, lemon juice and zest, chives, and dill. Use a fork, not a food processor — the point of a rillette is texture, visible flakes of fish bound loosely by fat, not a uniform paste. Season with salt, pepper, and hot sauce or cayenne if you want a background heat. Taste it cold, since that's how it'll be eaten: it should hit bright and a little sharp before the richness of the butter catches up. If it tastes flat, it needs more lemon, not more salt.

Pack into a lidded jar or container, smooth the surface, and press plastic wrap directly against it before sealing — this keeps the top from drying out and discoloring in the fridge.

### Toast the rye
Brush the rye squares with olive oil on both sides and grill or pan-toast over medium heat until deeply golden and a little charred at the edges, 2–3 minutes per side. Let cool completely before storing — warm bread sealed in a container steams itself soft. Fully cooled and stored airtight, it holds its crunch for hours, which is the whole point here.

---

## Assembly / To Serve

Don't assemble ahead of time. Set out the jar of rillettes, the toasted rye, and the pickled shallots, and let people build their own: a generous smear of rillettes on toast, two or three pickled shallot rings, a dill frond, a crack of pepper. The contrast of cold rich fish against hot-pink acidic shallot and crisp toast is the dish — plating it in advance just gets you soggy bread.

---

## Tips & Notes

- **Budget swap:** Smoked mackerel runs noticeably cheaper than smoked trout and brings the same big smoky character — swap it in 1:1 with zero apology. Trout is the default here for its milder, more universally-liked flavor, but if mackerel is what's fresh and affordable, use it.
- **Skip the crème fraîche.** Cream cheese does the same binding job for less money — there's no reason to buy both. If you already have crème fraîche, swapping it for half the cream cheese adds a touch more tang, but treat it as an upgrade, not a requirement.
- **Fish quality matters more than anything else in this recipe.** This dish has nowhere to hide a bad piece of fish — buy the best smoked trout your fishmonger has, not the shrink-wrapped supermarket version if you can avoid it.
- **Make ahead:** Rillettes and pickled shallots both keep refrigerated for up to 2 days, and the flavor of both improves overnight.
- **Boat-day transport:** Rillettes jar in the cooler, toasted rye in a separate dry container, pickled shallots in their jar. Keep everything sealed and separate until you're at the table.
- **Wine:** A dry, high-acid white — Muscadet, Sancerre, or a crisp Grüner Veltliner — cuts the richness and matches the pickle without fighting the smoke.
