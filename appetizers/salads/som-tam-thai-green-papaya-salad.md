# Som Tam — Thai Green Papaya Salad

*The most precisely balanced salad in the world: sour, salty, hot, and sweet in exact, vivid tension. Made in a mortar. Eaten immediately.*

**Serves:** 2
**Prep:** 20 min | **Cook:** 0 min | **Total:** 20 min

---

## Ingredients

### Salad
- 1 medium green papaya (about 500g / 1 lb), peeled and julienned into long strands
- 8–10 cherry tomatoes, halved
- 8 long beans (or green beans), cut into 5 cm / 2-inch pieces
- 2–4 Thai bird's eye chilies (2 = moderate heat; 4 = assertively spicy)
- 2 cloves garlic, roughly chopped
- 2 tbsp roasted peanuts, roughly crushed
- 1 tbsp dried shrimp — do not skip; they are the fermented umami backbone of this dish
- Fresh coriander, to garnish

### Dressing
- 2 tbsp fish sauce — quality is non-negotiable here; use Megachef, Tiparos, or a trusted Thai brand
- 2 tbsp fresh lime juice (from 1–2 limes, squeezed to order)
- 1 tbsp palm sugar, shaved or grated (light brown sugar if unavailable, but it is not the same)

---

## Method

### 1. Prepare the papaya
Peel the green papaya and discard the seeds. Using a papaya shredder, julienne peeler, or a sharp knife, cut the flesh into long strands roughly 2–3mm wide — julienne, not grated. You need length and structure. Set aside.

### 2. Make the dressing
Dissolve the palm sugar into the lime juice and fish sauce, stirring until fully combined. Taste it now: it should be assertively sour, properly salty, with sweetness in the background. Adjust before it goes anywhere near the salad — more lime for brightness, more fish sauce for salt and depth, more sugar only if it's too sharp.

### 3. Pound — bruise, don't pulverize
In a large granite mortar, pound the garlic and chilies to a rough paste — textured, not smooth. Add the long beans and strike them 4–5 times: cracked, split, slightly wilted, but still holding shape. Add the dried shrimp and crush briefly to break them up. Now add the papaya strands and pour over about two-thirds of the dressing.

Work the salad with a combination of light pounding and folding — use the pestle to bruise the papaya so it absorbs the dressing, and a large spoon to fold and redistribute. The strands must remain intact and crunchy. If they're turning to mash, you've gone too far.

### 4. Add the tomatoes
Drop in the halved cherry tomatoes and give them two or three strikes — just enough to crack them and bleed their juice into the dressing. Add the remaining dressing. Taste again: the balance should land sour-first, salty second, heat present throughout, sweetness quietly underneath. Adjust if needed.

### 5. Plate and serve immediately
Transfer to a wide, shallow plate. Scatter crushed peanuts over the top and finish with torn coriander leaves. Serve within 10 minutes — the papaya softens quickly and the dish is over once it does.

---

## Tips & Notes

- **Green papaya:** Must be completely unripe — firm, pale-white flesh, zero sweetness. If unavailable, green mango or kohlrabi are the most credible substitutes.
- **Fish sauce:** The fish sauce is the entire salt component. It defines the dish. Do not use a cheap or generic brand — the acrid, chemical notes will follow you into every bite. Megachef and Tiparos are widely available.
- **Dried shrimp:** Available at any Asian supermarket. They keep for months in the fridge. Their fermented, oceanic depth is not replicable by anything else. This is not optional.
- **Palm sugar vs. white sugar:** Palm sugar has a rounded, slightly caramel sweetness that white sugar lacks. Worth sourcing. Light brown sugar is a better stand-in than white.
- **No mortar?** A large mixing bowl and a wooden spoon handle will approximate the technique — use the handle end to bruise the beans and papaya before dressing. The result is inferior but workable.
- **This dish does not hold.** Make it, serve it, eat it. The papaya weeps within 20 minutes and the crunch — which is the point of the dish — is gone.
- **Heat:** Two chilies is a warm burn. Four is Thai-restaurant spicy. Do not drop below two; the heat is not garnish, it is structural.
