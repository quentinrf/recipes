# Smoked Trout Rillettes with Rye Toast, Pickled Shallot & Dill

*Serves 6–8 | Prep: 20 min | Pickle: 30 min (or up to 2 days ahead)*

---

## Ingredients

### For the rillettes
- 8 oz smoked trout (skin and pin bones removed), flaked
- 4 oz cream cheese, room temp
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
- 1 tbsp prepared horseradish (adjust to taste)
- 1 tbsp lemon juice, plus zest of half the lemon
- 2 tbsp finely chopped chives
- 1 tbsp chopped dill, plus extra fronds for garnish
- Dash of hot sauce or pinch of cayenne (optional)
- Salt and black pepper to taste

### For the pickled shallots
- 1 large shallot, sliced into thin rings
- 1/2 cup white wine vinegar or rice vinegar
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp salt
- 1/4 cup water

### To serve
- 1 loaf rye bread, sliced and cut into small squares
- Olive oil, for brushing
- Extra dill fronds and a crack of black pepper for garnish

---

## Instructions

### 1. Pickle the shallots
Heat vinegar, sugar, salt, and water just until dissolved — no need to boil. Pour over the shallot rings in a small jar, cool, then lid and refrigerate. Ready in 30 minutes, better after a day or two.

### 2. Make the rillettes
Mash the cream cheese and butter together until smooth. Fold in the flaked trout, horseradish, lemon juice and zest, chives, and dill. Work it with a fork rather than a food processor — you want visible flakes of fish, not a paste. Season with salt, pepper, and hot sauce/cayenne if using. It should taste bright and a little sharp, with the butter carrying the richness.

### 3. Pack and chill
Pack the rillettes into a lidded jar or container, smooth the top, and press plastic wrap directly onto the surface before sealing to keep it from drying out. Refrigerate.

### 4. Toast the rye
Brush rye squares with olive oil and grill or pan-toast until golden and a little crisp at the edges. Cool completely and store airtight — it holds its crunch fine sitting out for a few hours.

---

## Tips & Notes

- **Budget swap:** Smoked mackerel is noticeably cheaper than smoked trout and delivers the same big smoky flavor — use it 1:1 if you want to trim cost without losing impact. Trout is the default here because it's slightly milder and more universally liked, but don't hesitate to swap if mackerel's what's fresh and affordable.
- **Skip the crème fraîche:** Cream cheese does the same binding job for less money — no need to buy both. If you happen to have crème fraîche on hand, swapping in half of it for the cream cheese adds a touch more tang, but it's an upgrade, not a requirement.
- **Make ahead:** Rillettes and pickled shallots keep refrigerated for up to 2 days — flavor actually improves overnight.
- **Boat-day assembly:** Keep the rillettes jar in the cooler, the toasted rye in a separate dry container, and the pickled shallots in their jar. Spread and top at the table — don't assemble ahead or the toast will go soft.
